What the host gives

The host is the assistant process. Through IAssistantHost it gives the assistant everything it can see and use; the AI, the tools, the voice and K-Desktop stay out of sight.

Drawing

One drawing system: surfaces with WPF's drawing API. Rendering.CreateSurface() gives a surface; Present(area, dc => …) draws a frame on it, in screen pixels. Only present when something changes: the last frame stays on screen (the host repeats it after the GPU is lost or a full-screen game ends). Rendering.FrameInterval is the length of a frame of the monitor.

The bubble

When the assistant says something (Communicate), the host draws the bubble with the text, pointing at BubbleAnchor (usually the top of its head; null when it is not visible) and following it while it moves, for as long as it takes to read. The assistant only plays its «talking» gesture, or nothing: the bubble shows anyway.

A middle click on the character opens the box to write to it (so does Write to … in its right-click menu): left clicks and double clicks are yours. Holding the left button down without moving it (450 ms) is talking to it: you get Down and Up, but no Click or drag. The right button never reaches you: it opens its menu.

Its own text

With "speech": "own" in the manifest you draw what it says yourself (a bubble of your own, a sign, words inside a cloud…). The host calls ShowSpeech(SpeechText) each time the answer grows while it is being written (Finished false), with all of it when it is ready (Finished true), and again with a later Until while your voice is still saying it; each call replaces the one before. Show it until HideSpeech comes: the host still keeps the timing, the voice and the chat.

Text is plain text and arrives whole even when long: cut it to what fits (all of it is in the chat). If you cannot show it now (hidden, in the middle of a manoeuvre, it does not fit), return false: the host shows its bubble for the rest of that answer. On its card the user can choose Always use K-Desktop's speech bubble, and then nothing reaches you.

Long answers

When the user asks for something long («explain it in detail», «make me a list») or the answer does not fit a bubble, the bubble, your own text and the voice get only a short summary; K-Desktop shows the whole text on a reading sheet next to your BubbleAnchor. Nothing to do on your side.

States

SetState(state, detail) says what the module is doing, to show it your way (the Lander lights its antenna and shows an icon; the template changes colour):

AssistantState
Idle Back to rest
Listening Its word, its key to talk or the long click: it is listening. With detail.Activity = Dictation, K-Desktop is typing what the user says
Thinking The AI is working on an answer
ThinkingHard The AI thinks it over, slowly: it may take half a minute
LoadingBrain Its model is being loaded into memory before it can think
NoBrain The order needed the AI and there is none; it comes right before Speaking, which says so (a shrug)
Speaking It is answering
AwaitingConfirmation A yes/no question is waiting for the user (the small window that asks for permission)
Busy One of its tools or a command is running: detail.Activity says which (SearchingWeb, ReadingWeb, Files, Notes, Memory, Weather, Music, Timer, Screen, Command…; an MCP server's or the user's own: Other with detail.Tool)
Sleeping

detail.Text says it in its language («Searching the web»), in case you write it yourself. Later versions may add activities: treat those you do not know as Other.

Sound

IAudio: Load (floating-point samples, mono or stereo, any rate) or LoadWav (from the package), Play with gain and pan, and Loop with live gain, pitch and pan (for an engine). The host mixes it all under the user's volume; the device is only open while something plays. With sound off, Enabled is false and everything plays silence.

Click areas

Pointer.CreateHitArea(id), each with its id, which arrives in PointerEvent.Area (with several —the body and some toys, say— give them different names; without an id it is body). Place it with a shape (HitShape) wherever the character is. Over the area the cursor is the one you say (Cursor: arrow, hand, grab) or one you draw: SetCursorImage(image, hotspot) with a BitmapSource in monitor pixels (Windows cursors are 32 px at 100 %; Kobii shows a brush while being brushed); null goes back to the usual one. OnPointer gets entering, leaving, clicks and drags.

Files dropped on it

Click areas take files dragged from the Explorer. They reach OnPointer as FilesOver (over it: the moment to reach out), FilesAway (they left without being dropped, or the user pressed Escape) and FilesDropped (the moment to catch them), with the full paths in PointerEvent.Files. It is only the animation: what to do with them is decided by K-Desktop. They are never taken as «move»: the file stays where it was.

While the files are over your assistant, K-Desktop hides the picture Windows draws under the cursor and leaves only a small label, «Give it to ». That picture reaches you in PointerEvent.Preview (a frozen BitmapSource in screen pixels; null if the source program gave none), to show it your way: smaller, in its hands, hanging from a beam… The Lander holds it with its tractor beam, Kobii raises it over its hands, assistants without code carry it over their head. Not showing it is fine too.

Keyboard and wheel

  • IKeyboard: IsDown always; Capture makes K-Desktop swallow those keys (pressed without Ctrl, Alt or Win, so shortcuts still work) while playing, and their changes reach KeyChanged. Release when done (they are also released if the assistant leaves, the process crashes or K-Desktop is paused).
  • SystemEvent.FastScroll arrives when the user turns the wheel hard (with the cursor position); each notch (SystemEvent.WheelTurned, with the window under the cursor) only with Pointer.WatchWheel = true: it costs a message per notch, so only while it matters.

Windows

IWindowServices: TryMove (a window's visible part, for frame-by-frame dragging) and TryCommand (minimise, maximise, restore, bring to front —those of an administrator process, and bringing to front, are done by K-Desktop for you—), GetCaptionButtons (from DWM), Thumbnail (a frozen picture of the window) and AppIcon (its program's icon).

The world

  • World.Current: the monitors (bounds, work area, scaling), the windows with their bounds and the taskbars; Changed when it changes. World.System: the cursor and its speed, seconds without keyboard or mouse, the window in front, full screen… OnSystemEvent for what happens: a window opened, moved, activated or closed, a notification, the user left or came back, full screen, power and battery, the theme, the hour striking, the recycle bin emptied (with where its icon is, or null)…
  • Room for everyone: say where your body is with BodyBounds (only the body: no particles, bubbles or effects; null when not visible). World.Others says where each of the others is and whether it is on its way somewhere (Moving). Before landing, stopping or walking to a place, look at World.TakenBy(rectangle): it gives the one that stays there, or null when it is free. Passing in front is fine; staying on top is not.
  • Music: with World.ListenToMusic(true), World.Music says whether music with a beat is playing (Playing; a voice or noise does not count), its beats per minute (Bpm) and the beat (BeatAt(tick.Now): it grows by one per beat and its fraction is the phase, 0 = the hit), to dance in time. The host reads each program's volume meter (nothing is recorded), without the assistants' own sounds. It takes some 3 s to find the beat. Stop listening when you are not visible.

Helpers

  • Variety<T>: not to do the same thing every time (entrances, reactions, idle animations). Each variant with its weight (Add(x, weight: 2) comes up twice as often; 0 = never) and its cooldown in seconds. Pick() picks at random by weight, never the same twice in a row while there is another, and if all are resting gives the one ready soonest; TryPick gives nothing then. Pick(x => …) picks only among those that fit now. Use, CooldownLeft, Reset; with a seed the sequence is always the same (tests).
  • Rope: a Verlet rope in screen pixels for hooks, cables and leashes. You hold its Start; its end hangs (ReleaseEnd, with the speed you let it go at) or is held (PinEnd). It only resists stretching, never goes past MaxLength with a loose end and steps at a fixed 120 Hz (Step(tick.Elapsed)), so it behaves the same at any frame rate. Length to reel in or let out, Gravity, Damping, IsTaut, Overstretch, Pull (what a towed window follows). Draw(dc, pen) draws it; Bounds(margin) gives the size of the surface.
